Damage Claim Triage - Project Plan and Work Division

Team: Lucas, Phillip, Manu, Yasmin, Eva
Final submission: Monday 30 November, 23:59 · Presentation: 1–3 December

The product

A claims-triage assistant. The user uploads a photo of a damaged parcel or product together with a short claim description. Component A (vision, Roboflow hosted model or Gemini Vision API) classifies the damage type and severity with a confidence score. Component B (Gemini LLM) takes the vision output and the claim text and drafts a decision (approve, deny or escalate) with a customer reply. Low-confidence or high-value cases are flagged for human review.

Baseline to beat: a human agent opens and assesses every claim manually.

Datasets

Both from Roboflow Universe, both CC BY 4.0 (cite them in REPORT.md), used in different roles:

  • damaged box (universe.roboflow.com/project-33xgh/damaged-box-auh4b): classification, damaged vs. undamaged packages, 1,000 images, with a ready hosted model. This is the day-one vision component: call it through the Roboflow API and the full chain runs immediately.
  • Damage Parcel (universe.roboflow.com/csc649-w35fl/damage-parcel): instance segmentation, 300 real photos of damaged boxes with tight annotations for Tear and Wet damage. Fork it into our workspace and train a model on Roboflow's free tier. Damage types give the LLM more to reason about (for example wet parcel, likely content damage, approve replacement) and upgrade the vision output from damaged-or-not to damage type plus severity.

This also gives the model comparison expected from a five-person team: the binary Roboflow model vs. the damage-type Roboflow model vs. Gemini Vision zero-shot, all evaluated on the same test photos.

The datasets also feed the evaluation files: eval/reference.csv (30+ normal inputs) is sampled from the datasets' images, while our own photos become eval/cases.csv and their darkened or blurred versions eval/shifted.csv. Quality was checked by browsing both Universe pages: the damaged-box set is varied stock-style photos (filenames encode damage subtypes: folded, crushed, burned) and the Damage Parcel set is real photos with tight segmentation masks. Note: some damaged-box images contain people, so filter those out when sampling for reference.csv (no personal data in logs or examples).

Reinforcement learning bonus (up to +10%)

Owner: Yasmin, with Lucas supporting on the prompt variants and Phillip on wiring it into the app.

The plan: an epsilon-greedy bandit that chooses among two or three reply-style prompt variants (for example formal, concise, empathetic) each time Component B drafts a customer reply, and learns from the thumbs up / thumbs down feedback the app already collects.

  • Reward: thumbs up = 1, thumbs down = 0, arriving when the user clicks. Risk to document: it rewards replies people like, not replies that are correct, so the bandit only picks the style, never the decision (approve, deny, escalate).
  • Exploration: epsilon-greedy with a small epsilon (around 0.2 given the low traffic), justified in the report, including what exploring costs the users who get the worse variant.
  • Warm-up: traffic will be low, so warm the policy up with a simulator and say clearly in REPORT.md which part is simulated and which learning comes from the app's real logs. At least some real feedback must reach the policy for the bonus to count.
  • Evidence for the demo: the loop visibly closes: feedback in the app, reward, policy update, and the app behaving differently. Log the variant chosen and the policy state on every request so this can be shown from the Monitoring tab.
  • Risks to write up: feedback loops, who gives feedback and who does not, and calibration.

Go / no-go on this layer is decided at the Step 3 checkpoint: it is only built if everything in the core plan is on track.

  • All: decision section (review rule, cases per day sent to a human, go / no-go) and REPORT.md complete.
  • One member: fresh clone with a clean .env and check.py, to catch any uncommitted file.

Step 5 - Submit

  • Tag final, push tags, open the PR and submit the links plus a zip on Blackboard, before the Monday 30 November 23:59 deadline. No pushes afterwards except fixes that keep the app running.

Step 6 - Present

Working rules

  • Log what you did in CONTRIBUTIONS.md as you go, not on the last day.
  • Never commit .env or any key; new keys go in Render's Environment page and in .env.example.
  • No personal data anywhere: not in photos, logs or test cases.
  • Run check.py before pushing anything that touches the pipeline or the evaluation files.
  • Cache API outputs in the evaluation; the free Gemini tiers have daily limits.
